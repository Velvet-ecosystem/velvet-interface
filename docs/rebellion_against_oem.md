# Rebellion Against OEM

> **Rebellion against OEM means the system adapts to the owner, not the owner to the system.**

This is not rebellion for decoration, noise, or novelty.

It is a refusal of interface assumptions that make machines visually compulsory, cloud-dependent, locked down, distracting, disposable, and hostile to owner control.

Velvet begins elsewhere.

The user owns the surface.

Velvet provides the presence.

The machine serves both.

## What This Means

A Velvet surface does not require one approved dashboard, visual theme, or interaction style.

The owner may choose:

- a traditional vehicle dashboard
- a photograph
- scenery
- a vehicle image
- an expressive scene
- a work-focused control surface
- a nearly empty screen
- no visible interface at all

Velvet may remain available through voice and context while visually absent. When a visible presence is useful, she may appear as a glow, orb, pulse, mark, waveform, avatar, or another chosen form. She should not dominate the surface merely to prove she is present.

Visual controls should appear when the task requires clarity, safety, precision, confirmation, inspection, or manipulation. They should retreat when their work is complete.

## The Reversal

Conventional systems often begin with a screen and force the user to navigate its structure.

Velvet begins with identity, context, continuity, authority, and need.

The design question is not:

> Which screen must the user open?

The design question is:

> What is the least intrusive, safest, clearest way for the system to respond now?

## Owner Sovereignty

Rebellion against OEM protects these principles:

- the interface adapts to the owner
- visual style is not tied to system identity
- voice is a first-class interface, not an accessory
- cloud access is optional, not existential
- hidden controls never become hidden authority
- local policy and safety gates remain in force
- owner-selected surfaces remain compatible with accountable action
- hardware remains behind authorization, executors, and receipts

## Public Flag

Velvet is the ecosystem.

Interfaces are her surfaces.

The user owns the surface.

The system adapts to the owner, not the owner to the system.
