# Ambient Presence Doctrine

Velvet is not a screen.

A screen is one temporary body Velvet may use when speech alone is insufficient.

The default computer presence is ambient, voice-first, and capable of remaining visually absent. A visible interface appears only when the user requests it or when clarity, safety, precision, inspection, or manipulation requires it.

## Core Rule

Invisible by default. Visible by need or preference.

Velvet may be present through:

- voice recognition and spoken response
- a hidden background service
- a minimal glow, pulse, orb, mark, or waveform
- an image-based surface with mapped press points
- a focused panel for confirmation, status, control, or receipts
- a full task interface where the work is inherently visual

No single visual form is mandatory across all bodies.

## Surface Model

Each visible surface is composed from independent layers:

### 1. Surface Layer

The surface supplies the local visual body.

It may contain:

- a user-selected background image
- mapped polygon or press-point regions
- surface-specific widgets
- local body state
- an entry and exit transition profile

A surface image is not merely decoration. Its mapped regions route user intent into the Velvet event and authorization path.

### 2. Velvet Presence Layer

The presence layer is optional and remains independent from the surface beneath it.

Possible states include:

- hidden
- idle
- listening
- thinking
- speaking
- attention requested
- confirmation required
- warning
- denied
- degraded

The visual expression may be a glow, orb, pulse, shimmer, eye, candle, waveform, avatar, or another user-selected form. It must not become the sole interface or conceal required safety information.

### 3. Transition Layer

The transition layer moves between surfaces while preserving Velvet's identity and conversational state.

A transition may fade, dissolve, slide, dim, bloom, fold, or use a surface-specific effect. The visual body may change completely while Velvet's continuity remains intact.

## Visual Escalation

The interface should escalate only as far as the task requires:

1. voice only
2. minimal presence indicator
3. transient status or choice card
4. focused control or receipt panel
5. full visual surface

The interface should retreat when the need has passed.

## Surface Honesty

Different bodies require different amounts of interface.

- Desktop computers may remain almost entirely voice-first.
- Home installations may use voice, room displays, light, and contextual feedback.
- Vehicle surfaces must keep safety-critical state glanceable.
- Mobile surfaces must support silent use, remote requests, and clear authority limits.
- Foundry surfaces require rich visual tools for CAD, slicing, printers, queues, and machine state.
- Business surfaces require documents, approvals, tables, agent state, and receipts.

Consistency comes from Velvet's identity, voice, permissions, state language, event flow, and receipts. It does not require identical screens.

## Authority Boundary

Ambient presence does not grant authority.

A wake phrase, voice match, visible orb, hidden press point, passenger presence, or surface transition may begin an interaction, but none may bypass identity, policy, capability, safety, execution, or receipt requirements.

Correct flow:

    voice / press point / widget / gesture
      -> intent event
      -> identity and context check
      -> policy authorization
      -> capability and safety gates
      -> approved executor
      -> receipt

## Public Doctrine

The surface may change completely. Velvet does not.

Velvet appears only as much as the moment requires.
